For fixed positive lower density and expansion constants, there is a length threshold. For each larger length, positive error and weight constants can be chosen before the separation parameter. The actual degree, two-path, large-cut and separation hypotheses give positive weights on all canonical separated marked cycles with exactly one marked arc. The weights have order \(n^{2-c}\) and exactly realise every supplied balanced vector sufficiently close to the all-one vector. The proof establishes approximation uniformly for every nearby balanced vector, then proves the positive exact correction. It also checks that distinct canonical cycles have distinct incidence vectors and that injectivity of the physical edge map gives distinct physical edges on each cycle.

Manuscript label: thm:capacity:marked-numerical.

Proof ▼

The position splitting and copied-cycle construction supply exact weights before separation. Deleting non-separated cycles loses at most the explicit fixed coefficient times \((2b+1)/n\) in each edge equation. A positive baseline on every admissible cycle and the proved subspace coefficient theorem give exact positive coefficients. The size threshold is chosen after \(b\) to make the displayed error smaller than one quarter of the correction radius.

Integral cycle decompositions give fractional decompositions. Fractional weights on a family of subgraphs covering every edge with the same positive multiplicity can be averaged to give weights on the original graph. Theorem B.3 Ordinary cycle fractional threshold

For every fixed \(r\ge 3\) and \(\alpha {\gt}0\), every sufficiently large graph of minimum degree at least \((\delta _{C_r}+\alpha )n\) has a fractional \(C_r\)-decomposition. No divisibility assumptions are needed here.

Manuscript label: eq:threshold:cycle-fractional.

Proof ▼

Choose an even multiple \(q\) of \(r\) before the order threshold. One common colouring event controls all class degrees and all ordered colour–reserve paths. Recolour distinct reserve edges to correct edge-count residues; then pair the odd-degree vertices and switch along physically edge-disjoint paths to correct all degree parities. The reserve complement is even because \(q\) is even. Quantitative degree losses are absorbed at the fixed positive margin. Each of the \(q+1\) complements is \(C_r\)-divisible and meets the ordinary decomposition threshold. Averaging their decompositions covers each original edge with its exact multiplicity \(q\).

For each fixed cycle length and positive degree margin, there are positive constants and an order threshold, chosen before the host graph. Every supplied edge vector sufficiently close to one has exact positive cycle coefficients bounded above and below by constants times \(n^{2-r}\). In particular, the all-one vector gives a fractional cycle decomposition. The total weight through two incident edges is \(O(1/n)\), and through two disjoint edges it is \(O(1/n^2)\), for these same coefficients.

Manuscript label: thm:four:dense-spread.

Proof ▼

Sample a fixed number of vertices and independently retain physical edges with probabilities determined by the supplied vector. The successful induced graph has a fractional decomposition by the proved cycle threshold. Conditional-root concentration bounds the failed samples containing each specified edge. Average using the exact two-vertex inclusion probability; the \(r\)-vertex inclusion probability bounds each coefficient. The sample size is fixed before the host graph so that the approximation error is smaller than the positive correction radius. The proved convex coefficient theorem, with a positive baseline on every actual cycle, makes the edge loads exact. Injective encodings of physical cycles give the two pair-load bounds. This formal proof establishes the needed ordinary cycle result directly; it does not assume the general balanced-decomposition theorem quoted in the manuscript.

The admissible induced-subgraph and transfer-configuration counts in the manuscript imply an exact positive ordinary wheel weighting. The tolerance is chosen before the host graphs and initial weights. Every final wheel weight lies between \(b_-n^{1-r}/2\) and \((b_++b_-/2)n^{1-r}\).

Manuscript label: thm:pr:ordinary-wheel-weights.

Proof ▼

Lift the proved signed edge isolator to wheels at each admissible hub and average over the actual configurations. Count configurations containing the fixed rim together with the isolated edge to bound every coefficient. Average over its configurations and over pairs of neighbouring hubs. Only pairs containing the fixed wheel’s hub contribute, cancelling the normalization by the spoke degree. Sum the resulting bounds and choose the tolerance to preserve the positive baseline on every wheel.

An ordinary \(r\)-wheel has exactly \(2r\) physical edges, and different wheels have different edge sets. Restricting the hypergraph vertices to actual host edges gives exact fractional degree one and pair load at most \(r^4B/n\) whenever the wheel coefficients are at most \(Bn^{1-r}\). On a fixed parent wheel family with these coefficients, changing at most \(d\) incident edges per vertex changes a root-omitted potential load by at most \(8r^4Bd/n\) for a rim root and \(4r^4Bd/n\) for a spoke root. The root need not be present in either host. This node does not assert the isolator-configuration or transfer-configuration potential estimates.

Manuscript label: eq:loads:ordinary-potential-seed.

Proof ▼

Injective encodings bound cycles through prescribed vertices; the fixed hub and free hub cases give the actual wheel pair loads. The potential load is a product over every physical edge except the root. Its difference is bounded by the total weight through the root and each changed edge. For changed edges meeting root vertices use the \(n^{-1}\) pair bound; for other changed edges use \(n^{-2}\). Sum these bounds using the actual row and column degrees of the two difference relations. Candidate families, coefficients and root omissions remain fixed throughout.

Theorem B.8 Alternating corrections from intersection estimates
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.sparse_alternating_correction

For every positive lower density bound there are positive constants \(\delta _0,C\) such that the degree, pair-intersection, triple-intersection and exceptional weighted-codegree hypotheses of the manuscript imply: every edge vector with zero row and column sums is a linear combination of the alternating vectors of actual ordered four-cycles. The sum of the absolute coefficients incident with each edge is at most \(C\| z\| _\infty \). The constants precede the graph, supplied density profile, error and exceptional sets.

Manuscript label: lem:four:sparse-alternating.

Proof ▼

The actual four-corner averaging operator is written as \(T=I+K+L\). The two negative contributions cancel by the zero column and row sums. The weighted-codegree, degree and pair-intersection estimates bound \(L\). Expanding \(K^2\) gives the actual triple-intersection kernel. Repeated column indices and exceptional row pairs are bounded separately, giving an error proportional to \(\delta +D/|A|+1/|B|\). The support factor of \(K\) is retained on missing edges.

The operator \(E=I-T\) preserves the zero-marginal subspace and satisfies \(\| E^2z\| \le \| z\| /2\). In the formal proof, finite-dimensional linear algebra replaces the manuscript’s infinite series: this bound makes \(T\) injective and therefore surjective on that subspace. If \(Tw=z\), the identity \(w=z+Ez+E^2w\) bounds \(\| w\| \) by \(2(1+\| E\| )\| z\| \). The four-cycle coefficient bounds for \(Tw\) give the claimed incident load. No extra intersection assumption is introduced.

The marked numerical host estimates yield positive exact wheel weights. The length threshold precedes the cycle length; all coefficient and error constants precede the separation parameter and the host order. The spoke profiles are the supplied constant densities. The common-path family uses root-first coordinates and omits the four root spokes. These spokes are supplied only by the four-cycle used in the correction.

Manuscript label: thm:weights:marked-numerical.

Proof ▼

The proved marked capacity theorem corrects rim loads. Averaging cycles compatible with two hubs corrects spoke column sums. The remaining spoke residuals have zero row and column sums. The proved sparse alternating correction expresses each residual by actual four-cycles. A single common path tuple, used with both roots and both hubs, supplies its four-wheel transfer. The exact signed spoke equations and zero rim equations are proved on that same tuple family. Counting only four-cycles incident with the fixed wheel’s designated position bounds each resulting coefficient. The tolerance is then chosen to retain half of the initial lower bound.

The root-first orders are explicitly permuted into canonical cycle coordinates. The ordinary-root order places the marked arc from the second to the third free label. The conversion is injective and does not require separation of the two roots. Separation and physical-map injectivity give loopless, distinct physical rim edges and the cross-collision condition. The physical load identities, with disjoint spoke types, identify the typed exact equations with the manuscript’s physical edge equations. Constant spoke intersection estimates imply the inverse-density estimates by an explicit normalization, with the original exceptional sets preserved.
