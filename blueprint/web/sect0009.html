Definition 8.1 The finite sample spaces

For a finite set \(V\) of size \(D\), the subset space consists of all \(r\)-subsets, where \(r\le D\). An ordered partition with prescribed sizes \(d_1,\ldots ,d_k\) is represented by a function \(c:V\to [k]\) with \(|c^{-1}(a)|=d_a\). The sum condition \(\sum _a d_a=D\) supplies a partition, including when some cells are empty. Each space has its uniform probability law. A transposition of two vertices either leaves the object unchanged or performs one exchange in the manuscript’s sense.

Lemma 8.2 Exact laws under ordering and forgetting order

A uniform injection \([r]\to V\) has a uniform first label and, conditional on that label, a uniform continuation without replacement. Forgetting the order gives a uniform \(r\)-subset. Applying a uniform permutation to any one partition gives a uniform partition with the same cell sizes.

Proof ▼

The first-label decomposition is an explicit equivalence, and its fibres have equal cardinality. The fibre of a subset is equivalent to the injections \([r]\to S\), so all subset fibres have equal cardinality. For partitions, bijections between corresponding cells give a permutation carrying any partition to any other. Composition by that permutation is a bijection between the corresponding fibres of the sampling map. All statements identify point masses of the actual finite laws.

Theorem 8.3 Concentration for independent samples and partitions

Let independent coordinates be uniform subsets or partitions as above. Suppose one exchange in coordinate \(i\) changes \(f\) by at most \(b_i\ge 0\). Put \(s_i=\min (r_i,D_i-r_i)\) for a subset and \(s_i=D_i\) for a partition, and put \(v=\sum _i s_i b_i^2\). Then

\[ \mathbb E\exp \bigl(t(f-\mathbb Ef)\bigr)\le \exp (t^2v/8) \quad (t\in \mathbb R). \]

For \(v{\gt}0\) and \(a\ge 0\), the two-sided tail is at most \(2\exp (-2a^2/v)\), and each one-sided tail is at most \(\exp (-2a^2/v)\). If \(v=0\), \(f\) equals its expectation at every outcome.

Manuscript label: lem:simp:product-sampling.

Proof ▼

Expose an ordered sample one label at a time. A transposition couples the continuations of any two next labels, and changes the completed object by at most one exchange. Hence the two conditional means differ by at most \(b_i\). Conditional Hoeffding bounds and induction give the exponential-moment estimate. Complementation of subsets gives the smaller exposure count. Uniform permutations give the partition estimate. A separate product induction adds the contributions of independent coordinates. Exponential Markov bounds give the tails. In the zero case, strictness of \(1+x\le e^x\) gives equality at every outcome of positive mass; all these uniform product laws have positive mass everywhere. No independent-inclusion law is substituted for sampling without replacement.

Lemma 8.4 Exact subset inclusion and intersection means

For a uniform \(r\)-subset \(S\) of an \(n\)-element set and a fixed set \(T\),

\[ \Pr (T\subseteq S)=\frac{\binom r{|T|}}{\binom n{|T|}} \le (r/n)^{|T|}. \]

For every fixed \(A\), \(\mathbb E|A\cap S|=|A|r/n\). The formulas include \(r=0\) and the empty ground set, with Lean’s zero-division convention in the latter case; then \(A=T=\varnothing \).

Proof ▼

The fibre is counted by choosing the remaining elements outside \(T\). The factorial identity for binomial coefficients gives the displayed ratio. Expressing the ratio through descending factorials bounds each factor by \(r/n\). Summing the singleton probabilities gives the mean.

Let \(X\) be either a sum of independent finite random variables in \([0,1]\) or \(|S\cap A|\) for a uniform fixed-size subset. Put \(a=\mathbb EX\). For \(0\le \varepsilon \le 1\),

\[ \Pr (|X-a|\ge \varepsilon a)\le 2e^{-\varepsilon ^2a/3}. \]

If \(a{\gt}0\) and \(t\ge a\), then \(\Pr (X\ge t)\le (ea/t)^t\). When \(a=0\), \(X=0\) at every outcome of positive mass. This covers finite random variables; it does not assert a new proof for arbitrary infinite probability spaces.

Manuscript label: lem:prelim:chernoff.

Proof ▼

Convexity gives \(\mathbb E e^{sX}\le \exp (a(e^s-1))\) for the independent sum. For a uniform subset, expand the generating function as a sum of joint-inclusion probabilities. The preceding lemma gives \(\mathbb E q^{|S\cap A|}\le (1+(r/n)(q-1))^{|A|}\) for \(q\ge 1\). For \(0{\lt}q{\lt}1\), apply this bound to the complement subset with parameter \(q^{-1}\), retaining the binomial expression until the two formulas are identified. Setting \(q=e^s\) proves the same exponential-moment bound for every real \(s\). Exponential Markov bounds and proved scalar entropy inequalities give both tails and the large-deviation bound.

For a uniform \(r\)-subset \(S\) with \(r{\gt}0\) and fixed sets \(A_j\subseteq V\),

\[ \Pr \left(\forall j\in J:\left|\frac{|A_j\cap S|}{r} -\frac{|A_j|}{|V|}\right|{\lt}t\right) \ge 1-2|J|e^{-2rt^2}\qquad (t\ge 0). \]

The index type for \(j\) is arbitrary; it need not be a subset of \(V\). Thus these fixed tests may describe neighbourhoods of vertices outside the sampled domain. The graph-specific identification of the sets is still a caller obligation.

Proof ▼

A transposition adds at most one new element to a subset and removes at most one old element. Hence a fixed intersection count changes by at most one, and the normalized count changes by at most \(1/r\). Its mean is \(|A_j|/|V|\). The ordered-sample exposure bound with \(r\) steps gives the stated tail; a union bound gives the common event. If its displayed lower bound is positive, a successful subset exists.
