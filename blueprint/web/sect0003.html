Theorem 2.2 Uniformisation from the fixed-set theorem alone

The fixed-set target implies the uniform target.

Proof ▼

Fix \(\alpha {\gt}0\) and put \(b=\alpha /2\) and \(M=\lceil 1/b\rceil \). For each integer \(0\le q\le M\), let

\[ T_q=\{ r\ge 3:\delta _r\le qb\} . \]

If \(T_q\) is nonempty, take its fixed-set order threshold at margin \(b\); otherwise use zero. Take the maximum before choosing \(S\). For a later nonempty \(S\), put \(\theta =\delta _S\) and \(q=\lceil \theta /b\rceil \). The proved bounds \(0\le \theta \le 1\) give \(q\le M\) and \(\theta \le qb\le \theta +b\). Consequently \(S\subseteq T_q\) and \(\delta _{T_q}\le qb\le \delta _S+b\). The degree assumption for \(S\) at margin \(\alpha \) implies that for \(T_q\) at margin \(b\), without changing any prescribed factor. This reduction does not require an estimate as the cycle length tends to infinity. The fixed-set target remains unproved.

Theorem 2.3 The manuscript’s alternative common-tail reduction

Assume the fixed-set target, the bounds \(\delta _r\ge 1/2\) for \(r\ge 3\), and the common-tail property. Then the uniform target holds. It suffices for the tail property that, for every \(\alpha {\gt}0\), all large \(r\) satisfy \(\delta _r\le 1/2+\alpha /2\).

Proof ▼

For every \(B\subseteq E\), take a fixed-set threshold for \(T\cup B\) at margin \(\alpha /2\). Take the maximum of these finitely many integers before choosing \(S\). For any later \(S\), put \(B=S\cap E\) and \(S^*=T\cup B\). Then \(S\subseteq S^*\) and

\[ \delta _{S^*}\le \delta _S+\alpha /2. \]

The degree hypothesis at margin \(\alpha \) for \(S\) therefore implies the hypothesis at margin \(\alpha /2\) for \(S^*\). The prescribed factors are unchanged. The Lean proof explicitly constructs the finite maximum and checks the supremum inequalities and quantifier order.

The fixed-set result and the lower and cofinite upper threshold estimates are hypotheses of the common-tail reduction. The grid reduction above removes the latter two hypotheses from the uniformisation step. Neither reduction proves the fixed-set theorem.
