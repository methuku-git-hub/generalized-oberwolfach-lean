Theorem 10.1 Brouwer fixed point
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.compact_convex_fixed_point

A continuous self-map of a nonempty compact convex subset of a finite-dimensional real normed space has a fixed point.

Manuscript label: thm:prelim:brouwer.

Proof ▼

The vendored proof from harfe/fixed-point-theorems-lean4 is ported to the pinned library. Its cubical Sperner argument and finite-dimensional homeomorphism reduction are included in the kernel check. No fixed-point axiom is introduced. The MIT license and source retrieval and port records accompany the source.

The fixed-marginal transportation domains are nonempty compact convex sets. For actual consecutive interfaces, the reserve respects every edge capacity and the scaled residual endpoint matrix belongs to the same domain. This map is continuous, including at zero residual degree.

Manuscript label: doLpart:eq:reserve-weight.

Manuscript label: doLpart:eq:H-map.

Proof ▼

Prove the reserve-load bound using the common-neighbourhood lower bound. Exact residual row and column sums give the prescribed marginals. At zero residual degree, bound the path weight between zero and that degree. No division by a possibly zero number is cancelled.

Theorem 10.4 Fractional cycles in the interface representation
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 10.3

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.cyclicInterfaceGraph_has_fractional_cycles

For \(k\ge 2\), specify the interfaces \(X_iY_i\) and \(Y_iX_{i+1}\) by adjacency relations. Assume local balance at both types of vertex, nonempty finite classes, and endpoint degrees at least \(\delta \), where \(k|Y_i|\le (2k-1)\delta \) and all middle classes have the same order. Then the resulting graph has a fractional decomposition into actual part-respecting copies of \(C_{2k}\). The formal representation uses copies of fixed finite types \(X,Y\) for the classes. Transport from an arbitrary named partition of a host vertex set is a separate semantic bridge.

Manuscript label: doLpart:thm:main.

Proof ▼

Local balance proves the common interface mass. Apply the fixed-point path construction with marginals calculated from the given graph. Reserve plus residual weights recover both interface capacities exactly. Tag the classes and embed the cycle injectively, checking every consecutive edge. Positive-weight tuples lie in the host graph. Aggregate weights over all tuples representing each actual subgraph; the edge equations are preserved, including zero loads on nonedges.

Corollary 10.5 Positive weights and pair loads in the cyclic graph
✓
#

Uses

  • Theorem 10.4

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.cyclicInterfaceGraph_fractional_cycles_with_pair_bounds

With equal class sizes \(n\) and minimum cyclic degree at least \((k/(2k-1)+\alpha )n\), the same construction gives weights on actual part-respecting cycles between \(a_-n^{2-2k}\) and \(a_+n^{2-2k}\), where \(a_-,a_+{\gt}0\) depend only on \(k,\alpha \). The total weight through two distinct adjacent edges is at most \(a_+/n\), and through two vertex-disjoint edges at most \(a_+/n^2\). The formal statement uses tagged copies of a common finite type.

Manuscript label: doLpart:cor:spread.

Proof ▼

Preserve the explicit conditional-product formula through the existence construction. Positive slack bounds all residual path weights on both sides and makes every residual endpoint entry positive. Cancel the endpoint weights in each sector, including the empty internal product for four-cycles. Compatible tuples have unique cycle-graph descriptions, so aggregation introduces no multiplicity factor. Count selections with prescribed vertices by their fixed coordinates; conflicting prescriptions in one class give zero selections. Apply this count to the three or four endpoints of the specified edges.

Lemma 10.6 Regularization with controlled edge-deletion probabilities
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.retainedBipartiteGraph_deleted_edge

For fixed \(\beta {\gt}0\), choose \(C,\eta _0{\gt}0\), then for each \(0{\lt}\eta {\lt}\eta _0\) choose \(m_0\). A bipartite graph with equal parts of size \(m\ge m_0\), minimum degree \((1/2+\beta )m\) and degrees in \([d-\eta m,d+\eta m]\) has a random spanning \(\lfloor d-\sqrt\eta m\rfloor \)-regular subgraph in which each original edge is deleted with probability at most \(C\sqrt\eta \). The formal graph uses two tagged copies of the label type.

Manuscript label: lem:four:random-regularization.

Proof ▼

Prove the required large-cut estimate from the minimum degree. Explicit floor and scale inequalities give the scaled demands used by the real-flow theorem. For rounding, construct a doubly stochastic auxiliary matching matrix with degree slots and a private complement for each original edge. Birkhoff’s theorem gives a finite law with the prescribed edge marginals. Supported matchings satisfy the exact integer demands. Their complementary original edge sets give the regular graph. No independence among deleted edges is asserted.

Lemma 10.7 Conditional partition degree estimates
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.fixedPartition_two_placement

Condition on prescribed classes for a fixed vertex set \(X\). For selected classes of size \(m\), a fixed family \(J\) of vertex permutations, and every vertex \(v\), test the numbers of labels \(z\) in a class for which \(v\sigma (z)\) is an edge. If \(\varepsilon \le \eta /4\), \(\eta \le 1\) and \(2|X|\le \eta m/4\), all these counts differ from \(Dm/n\) by less than \(\eta m\) on one event of probability at least

\[ 1-2|\mathcal C||J|n\exp (-\eta ^2m/2). \]

The two-label placement probability in two distinct classes is exactly \(m^2/(n(n-1))\).

Manuscript label: thm:four:successor-kernel.

Proof ▼

The remaining labels have the exact uniform fixed-size partition law. A cell therefore has the uniform fixed-size subset law. Bound the conditional mean shift by \(2|X|\) and apply the proved exchange concentration inequality. Union-bound the fixed graph-neighbourhood and inverse-image tests. The vertices in these tests range over the entire original domain. Count labelled injections to obtain the exact placement probabilities.

Lemma 10.8 Positive weights after cyclic interface regularization
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.stochastic_cyclic_weights

Near-regular cyclic zero-one interface matrices admit nonnegative weights on their transversal tuples. If their common regularized degree \(q=\lfloor d-\sqrt\eta m\rfloor \) exceeds the positive-margin cyclic threshold, every positional load is exactly \(q\). Every original interface edge has load between \(1-C\sqrt\eta \) and \(1\); nonedges have zero load. All tuple weights have a common upper bound \(B m^{2-c}\), and every valid tuple has weight at least \((1-cC\sqrt\eta )A m^{2-c}\), with the same positive cyclic constants.

Manuscript label: thm:four:successor-kernel.

Proof ▼

Sample the independently regularized interfaces and apply the proved positive cyclic weighting to each positive-mass outcome. Removing zero-mass outcomes changes no probabilities or expectations. Average these weights under the same finite law. Exact incidence equations give the positional and edge loads. A union bound over the specified tuple’s \(c\) interface edges gives its survival probability; no independence among edges of an interface is used.

Lemma 10.9 Weight lost by excluding close pairs
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.separated_successor_edges_injective

For tuples with \(c\) coordinates, fix \(a\) pairwise separated labels, where \(1\le a{\lt}c\). If each tuple has weight at most \(C n^{2-c}\), the total weight of tuples satisfying these prescriptions and having a pair at permutation distance at most \(b\) is at most

\[ c^2(2b+1)C/n^{a-1}. \]

A separated successor tuple has distinct physical edges, including its permuted closing edge.

Manuscript label: thm:four:successor-kernel.

Proof ▼

A violating pair must include a coordinate outside the prescribed set. Expose all other coordinates; the remaining label has at most \(2b+1\) choices. Sum over ordered pairs and apply the pointwise weight bound. For edge distinctness, use injectivity of the tuple labels and the separation of the successor of the first label from all other labels.
