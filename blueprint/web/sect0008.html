Lemma 7.1 Fixed root-omitted expectations
✓
#

Uses

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.FiniteLaw.pi
  • Oberwolfach.finiteLaw_expectation_pattern

For independent categorical coordinates with probability vectors \(p_e\), a prescribed pattern on \(F\) has probability \(\prod _{e\in F}p_e(s_e)\). For fixed coefficients, candidate families and omitted roots,

\[ \mathbb E\sum _{i\in C}w_i\prod _{e\in F_i\setminus R} \mathbf1_{\{ \sigma _e=s_{i,e}\} } =\sum _{i\in C}w_i\prod _{e\in F_i\setminus R}p_e(s_{i,e}). \]

The product law is explicitly defined and proved to have total mass one. No conditioning on root presence occurs. This does not prove the manuscript-specific identities for physical images or their influence counts.

Lemma 7.2 Projection of one common event
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.FiniteLaw.prod
  • Oberwolfach.finiteLaw_common_event_projection

Let colours and auxiliary directions have independent finite laws. If a joint event \(E\) implies that every later admissible family admits its required construction, then the probability of the colour event expressing this universal conclusion is at least the probability of \(E\).

Proof ▼

Sum the product masses over the joint event. For each colour outcome its fibre contributes at most the colour’s mass; outside the universal colour event the fibre is empty. This preserves the quantifier over all later families on one event. It does not bound the probability of the joint event.

Theorem 7.3 Concentration for the finite categorical product
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 7.1

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.boundedIndependentSum_tail
  • Oberwolfach.finiteLaw_pi_measure
  • Oberwolfach.finiteLaw_coordinate_independence
  • Oberwolfach.finiteLaw_bounded_coordinate_sum

For independent finite coordinates \(\sigma _i\), and functions \(a_i\le X_i(\sigma _i)\le b_i\),

\[ \Pr \left(\left|\sum _i X_i-\sum _i\mathbb E X_i\right|\ge t\right) \le 2\exp \left(-\frac{2t^2}{\sum _i(b_i-a_i)^2}\right) \qquad (t\ge 0). \]

The explicit finite product is identified with the product probability measure, proving coordinate independence. The bound follows from mathlib’s proved subgaussian Hoeffding estimate, applied to the centred variables. In a raw directed-colour cut, one physical edge has one categorical colour–direction state. If both directions meet the cut, its two contributions are combined into one \([0,1]\)-valued variable; its expectation is the sum of the two corresponding \(\rho _a/2\) terms. This theorem does not yet establish the simultaneous cut event or its asymptotic failure bound required by the protected partition.

Theorem 7.4 Bounded differences and fixed weighted patterns
✓
#

Uses

  • Oberwolfach.finiteLaw_boundedDifferences

Let \(X\) be a function of independent finite categorical coordinates. If changing only coordinate \(e\) changes \(X\) by at most \(b_e\ge 0\), then

\[ \mathbb E e^{u(X-\mathbb EX)}\le e^{u^2\sum _e b_e^2/8},\qquad \Pr (|X-\mathbb EX|\ge t)\le 2e^{-2t^2/\sum _e b_e^2}\quad (t\ge 0). \]

When all \(b_e=0\), \(X\) equals its expectation at every configuration. In particular, this applies to a fixed root-omitted weighted pattern sum with \(b_e\ge \sum _{i:e\in F_i\setminus R}w_i\). Its expectation is the exact product expression above; weights and omitted roots remain fixed.

Manuscript label: thm:prelim:bounded-differences.

Proof ▼

Induct on the number of coordinates. Averaging over the remaining coordinates changes the conditional mean by at most the first coordinate’s bound. The finite-variable Hoeffding exponential estimate controls this conditional mean; the induction hypothesis controls each conditional tail. Factoring the exponential and summing the product law gives the displayed moment bound. Apply the proved subgaussian tail inequality. Reindexing a finite coordinate set preserves its exact product law. The zero-influence case follows by changing coordinates one at a time. The weighted assertion uses the previously checked deterministic influence inequality.

Lemma 7.5 One event for all fixed tests
✓
#

Uses

If fixed bad events \(A_j\) have probabilities at most \(p_j\), then \(\Pr (\bigcap _j A_j^c)\ge 1-\sum _jp_j\). If the sum is less than one, a positive-mass outcome satisfies every test. Applying this to bounded differences keeps each test’s own tolerance and influence bounds. No independence between tests or uniformity over arbitrary outcome-dependent domains is assumed.

Lemma 7.6 Fair orientations and simultaneous two-step paths
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 7.5
  • Lemma 6.5

Lean declarations

Sample one fair bit for each unordered vertex pair; bits on nonedges are unused. This gives exactly one direction to each edge. For a fixed eligibility graph \(E\subseteq G\), the number of directed paths \(x\to z\to y\) with both edges in \(E\) has mean \(|N_E(x)\cap N_E(y)|/4\). For \(x\ne y\), different choices of \(z\) use disjoint physical coordinate pairs. The probability of at most \(|N_E(x)\cap N_E(y)|/8\) such paths is at most \(2\exp (-|N_E(x)\cap N_E(y)|/64)\). The corresponding degree bounds and all pair bounds hold on one event with the explicit sum of their failure bounds. On this event, the routing conclusion holds for every later forbidden graph satisfying its degree bound.

Lemma 7.7 Balanced orientation on a common event
✓
#

Uses

For every \(\alpha {\gt}0\) there is \(N(\alpha )\) such that the following holds for every graph \(G\) of order \(n\ge N(\alpha )\) with even degrees and \(\delta (G)\ge (1/2+\alpha )n\). A fair independent orientation \(D_0\) has, with probability at least \(1-n^{-10}\), this property: for every later graph \(F\) with \(\Delta (F)\le \alpha n/100\), there is a balanced orientation \(D\) of \(G\) which changes at most \(4n^{4/5}\) incident edges at any vertex and changes no edge of \(F\). The order threshold precedes the vertex set, graph and forbidden graph.

Manuscript label: clm:partition:balanced-orientation.

Proof ▼

Minimum degree gives at least \(2\alpha n\) common neighbours for every pair. The checked orientation event has failure at most

\[ 2n\exp (-2n^{1/5})+2n^2\exp (-\alpha n/32). \]

This is a valid weaker intermediate exponential estimate than the manuscript’s Chernoff estimate, with the same required final bound. The checked real-power asymptotics make it at most \(n^{-10}\). Set \(a=n^{3/5}\), \(\beta =\alpha /4\), \(f=\alpha n/100\) and \(L=\lceil n^{4/5}\rceil \). The proof includes all rounding inequalities, shows the routing margin for large \(n\), and proves \(a+2L\le 4n^{4/5}\). Evenness and the degree-sum identity supply the half-degree vector and its required total. The exact physical identity gives both the final indegree and outdegree. The forbidden graph is chosen after the event.

Lemma 7.8 The large-cut consequence of minimum degree
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.graphCut_lower_of_degrees

Define \(e_G(S,T)=\sum _{x\in S}|N_G(x)\cap T|\), counting ordered pairs. If \(\delta (G)\ge (1/2+\alpha )n\) and \(|S|+|T|{\gt}(1-2\delta )n\), then

\[ e_G(S,T)\ge (\alpha -\delta )n\min \{ |S|,|T|\} . \]

The count is symmetric in \(S,T\). If \(T\) is the larger side, it has size greater than \((1/2-\delta )n\), and each vertex in \(S\) has at least \(\delta (G)+|T|-n\) neighbours in \(T\). Summation proves the inequality; the other case follows by symmetry. Overlapping sides retain both ordered occurrences of an edge when appropriate.

Lemma 7.9 Simultaneous categorical directed-colour cuts
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 7.8

Lean declarations

Give each unordered pair one categorical state \((a,b)\) of probability \(\rho _a/2\), where \(\rho _a\ge 0\) and \(\sum _a\rho _a=1\). On graph edges, \(a\) is the colour and \(b\) the direction. The colour classes partition the physical edges exactly. For fixed \(S,T\), their directed-colour count \(X_a(S,T)\) has expectation \(\rho _a e_G(S,T)/2\). For any fixed number of colours \(k\) and \(q{\gt}0\), there is \(N(k,q)\) such that, for every graph of order \(n\ge N(k,q)\) and every such profile, with probability at least \(1-n^{-10}\),

\[ \left|X_a(S,T)-\rho _a e_G(S,T)/2\right|{\lt}qn^2 \quad \text{for every }a,S,T. \]

The threshold precedes the vertex set, host and profile. This absolute quadratic tolerance controls large cuts; it does not replace the separate small-cut argument using vertex degrees in the partition proof.

Manuscript label: eq:family:raw-partition-cut.

Proof ▼

Write the ordered incidences as fixed singleton-coordinate patterns. Their exact expectation follows from the categorical law. The fibre of an unordered pair among ordered pairs has size at most two, proving a coordinate influence bound of two without assuming independent opposite arcs. Bounded differences gives a failure at most \(2\exp (-t^2/(2n^2))\). Sum over the \(k4^n\) colour–cut tests and put \(t=qn^2\). The proof verifies \(2k4^n\exp (-q^2n^2/2)\le n^{-10}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). This supplies the simultaneous raw cut estimate; assembling the degree, protected-set, balancing and cut-loss events remains a separate obligation.
