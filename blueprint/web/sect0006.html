Theorem 5.1 Perfect matchings in a regular bipartite multigraph
✓
#

  • Oberwolfach.regularBipartite_perfectMatching

Let \(X,Y\) be finite sets and \(M:X\times Y\to \mathbb N\) the edge multiplicity function of a bipartite multigraph. If every row sum and column sum is \(d\), there are bijections \(\sigma _c:X\to Y\), indexed by \(c{\lt}d\), such that

\[ M(x,y)=\sum _{c{\lt}d}\mathbf1_{\{ \sigma _c(x)=y\} }. \]

When \(d=0\), the index set is empty; no equality of the sizes of \(X,Y\) is required in that case.

Proof ▼

For \(d{\gt}0\), double counting gives \(d|X|=d|Y|\). For every \(S\subseteq X\), counting the edges incident with \(S\) gives \(d|S|\le d|N(S)|\), so Hall’s theorem supplies a perfect matching. Subtract one from the multiplicity of each of its edges. Every row sum and column sum decreases by one. Induction on \(d\) gives the asserted identity, including parallel copies. The Lean proof uses mathlib’s proved finite Hall theorem.

If every row and column sum of \(M\) is at most \(d\), its individual edges

\[ E_M=\{ (x,y,i):x\in X,\ y\in Y,\ 0\le i{\lt}M(x,y)\} \]

admit a colouring with \(d\) colours such that two edges of the same colour sharing an endpoint are equal.

Manuscript label: thm:prelim:konig.

Proof ▼

Write \(r_x=\sum _yM(x,y)\) and \(s_y=\sum _xM(x,y)\). Complete \(M\) to a \(d\)-regular bipartite multigraph on \((X\sqcup Y,Y\sqcup X)\), with matrix

\[ \begin{pmatrix} M & \operatorname {diag}(d-r_x) \\ \operatorname {diag}(d-s_y) & M^{\mathsf T} \end{pmatrix}. \]

Apply the regular decomposition and restrict each matching to the original \(X\times Y\) block. For each pair \((x,y)\), the number of retained colours is exactly \(M(x,y)\). A bijection between these colours and its parallel-copy indices defines the edge colouring. The Lean proof establishes injectivity on each set of incident edges.

A \(kq\)-regular bipartite multigraph splits into \(k\) multigraphs with degree \(q\) on both sides. Suppose moreover that its two parts are copies of the vertex set of a simple graph \(G\), and

\[ M(x,y)+M(y,x)=\mathbf1_{\{ xy\in E(G)\} }\quad \text{for all }x,y. \]

Then grouping the perfect matchings into \(k\) groups of size \(q\) and forgetting directions gives an exact edge partition of \(G\) into \(2q\)-regular spanning graphs.

Manuscript label: lem:family:partition.

Proof ▼

Reindex the \(kq\) perfect matchings by \([k]\times [q]\) and sum within each group. If its resulting multiplicities are \(Q_a(x,y)\), put \(W_a(x,y)=Q_a(x,y)+Q_a(y,x)\). Then

\[ \sum _aW_a(x,y)=\mathbf1_{\{ xy\in E(G)\} },\qquad \sum _yW_a(x,y)=2q. \]

The first identity makes every \(W_a(x,y)\) either zero or one, excludes loops, and proves that each physical edge belongs to exactly one group. The second gives the degree. The Lean conclusion uses the same EdgePartition definition as the main target.
