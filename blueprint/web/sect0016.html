Lemma C.1 One common colouring outcome
✓

Let indexed edges have at most \(k\) resources and lists of size \(l\), and let the designated real edges have exactly \(k\) resources. Suppose each resource–colour degree is at most \(s\le l\), with equality for every colour in a real edge’s list at each of its resources. Write

\[ \rho =(1-1/l)^{ks},\qquad \kappa =1-s\rho /l. \]

Under the explicit codegree and exponential-tail inequalities in the Lean statement, one outcome of the original product experiment simultaneously approximates every real available-list size by \(l\kappa ^k\) and every root-omitted remaining colour degree by \(|U|(1-\rho )\kappa ^{k-1}\). The error is the stated expectation error plus \(6(k+1)t\). The retained colours belong to their supplied lists. Finite padding equalizes the original resource–colour degrees without increasing codegrees between distinct resources.

Proof ▼

The exact product law supplies all expectations and tails. Each test uses only two successive closed neighbourhoods of a specified base set. Bounding base-set sizes and their coordinate incidence gives one application of the finite local lemma. The same positive-mass outcome works for all tests. The proof retains the omitted resource in every remaining-degree test.

The finite iteration and its assembly into near-optimal hypergraph colouring are now proved below. The uniform choices for the fixed-error weighted theorem and its fractional-resource rounding application are also proved below.

Lemma C.2 Cycle weights for vertex-disjoint packing
✓
#

Uses

  • Theorem B.5

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.ordinaryCycleVertices_card
  • Oberwolfach.ordinary_cycle_vertex_weight_identity
  • Oberwolfach.ordinary_cycle_vertex_matching_weights

Suppose \(a_C\ge 0\) is an edge-fractional \(C_r\)-decomposition of \(Q\) with \(a_C\le B n^{2-r}\). The hyperedge indexed by \(C\) has resource set \(V(C)\), which has size \(r\). If \(d\ge n/2\) and \((1-\eta )d\le d_Q(v)\le d\), then \(b_C=2a_C/d\) satisfies

\[ 1-\eta \le \sum _{C\ni v}b_C\le 1,\qquad \sum _{C\ni x,y}b_C\le \frac{4r^2B}{n}\quad (x\ne y). \]
Proof ▼

Summing the edge equations at \(v\) counts each cycle containing \(v\) twice, so \(2\sum _{C\ni v}a_C=d_Q(v)\). Counting actual cycle subgraphs through two fixed vertices bounds their total \(a\)-weight by \(r^2B\). Rescaling and \(d\ge n/2\) give the assertions.

The almost-regular hypothesis is not supplied by every current application of the manuscript’s cycle-factor theorem. This lemma therefore does not by itself replace that theorem or establish the required absorber construction.

Lemma C.3 Bounded perfect fractional matchings
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.bounded_bipartite_fractional_matching
  • Oberwolfach.bounded_perfect_fractional_matching

If \(0{\lt}\beta {\lt}1/2\), \(m\ge 4/\beta \), and \(Q\) is an \(m\)-vertex graph with minimum degree at least \((1/2+\beta )m\), then \(Q\) has a perfect fractional matching with every edge coefficient in \([\beta /(5m),5/(\beta m)]\).

Manuscript label: lem:selection:bounded-fractional-matching (live-source amendment).

Proof ▼

The Lean proof uses the previously proved real-flow cut criterion on the bipartite double cover. A baseline \(\beta /(5m)\) is placed on every edge; the remaining row and column demands lie in \([1-\beta /5,1]\). The dense cut inequalities give an additional coefficient at most \(4/(\beta m)\) on each edge. Symmetrisation gives vertex sums one in \(Q\). The live manuscript instead contains the authors’ shorter Hall proof. Both proofs establish the same statement, without the cited literature lemma.

Theorem C.4 Fractional almost-perfect matching
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.almost_perfect_matching_of_fractional_weights

Let \(r\ge 2\), \(0{\lt}\xi \le 1\), and \(\mu =(\xi /(100r))^{20r}\). For a finite indexed \(r\)-uniform hypergraph, suppose nonnegative edge weights have vertex loads in \([1-\mu /4,1]\) and pair loads at most \(\mu /4\). Then a matching covers at least \((1-\xi )|V|\) vertices.

Proof ▼

Replace edge \(e\) by \(\lfloor Tw_e\rfloor \) separately indexed copies, where \(\mu T{\gt}4(|E|+1)\). The degree rounding loss is at most \(|E|\). With \(D_0=T\) and \(B=\lceil \mu T/2\rceil \), the checked matching theorem of Norin and Steiner applies. Its six-file dependency closure is included, with licence, pinned revision, and the compatibility patch recorded. Projection back to original indices preserves disjointness and coverage. The displayed constant is the one in the formal development, with an additional margin for rounding; it is not the larger paper constant. This theorem alone does not control several prescribed weight sums.

Theorem C.5 Almost-spanning cycle packing
✓
#

Uses

  • Theorem B.5
  • Lemma C.2
  • Theorem C.4

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.almost_spanning_cycle_packing

For \(r\ge 3\), \(\alpha {\gt}0\) and \(0{\lt}\xi \le 1\), there are \(\eta {\gt}0\) and \(N\) such that every graph \(Q\) of order \(n\ge N\) with \(\delta (Q)\ge (\delta _{C_r}+\alpha )n\) and \((1-\eta )d\le d_Q(v)\le d\) for all \(v\) has vertex-disjoint \(r\)-cycles covering at least \((1-\xi )n\) vertices.

Proof ▼

Use positive bounded fractional cycle weights, convert edge loads to vertex loads, and apply the preceding theorem. The order threshold bounds \(4r^2B/n\) by \(\mu /4\). All constants precede the graph and the degree scale. The conclusion is an approximate factor, not an exact factor or an absorber.

For fixed \(k,K,\delta ,\tau {\gt}0\) with \(K\ge 1\) and \(\delta \le 1\), one order threshold ensures all the explicit concentration conditions for every \(l\) above that threshold, every \(0{\lt}s\le l\), every codegree bound \(B\le K l^{1-\delta }\), and every dependency-degree bound \(d\le K l^2\). The robust ratio-progress and list-size inequalities are proved separately.

Proof ▼

The concentration denominator is at most \(A_k l(B+1)\) and the dependency factor is at most \((k+1)(K+1)^4l^{10}\). A stretched exponential in \(l^\delta \) dominates this polynomial uniformly over all supplied choices.
