Lemma 6.1 Integral prescribed degrees
✓
#

  • Oberwolfach.bipartiteEdges_filter_card
  • Oberwolfach.bipartite_prescribedMultiplicity

Let \(B\) be a finite bipartite multigraph with parts \(X,Y\), whose parallel edges are distinct elements of a finite set. Let \(f:X\to \mathbb N\) and \(g:Y\to \mathbb N\) have equal totals. If

\[ f(S)\le g(T)+e_B(S,Y\setminus T)\qquad (S\subseteq X,\ T\subseteq Y), \]

then a subset of the original edges has degrees \(f\) on \(X\) and \(g\) on \(Y\). The equivalent multiplicity-matrix conclusion also retains every capacity.

Manuscript label: lem:prelim:prescribed-degrees.

Proof ▼

For each \(x\), create \(f(x)\) slots adjacent to all edges incident with \(x\). For each \(y\), create \(d_B(y)-g(y)\) additional slots adjacent to all edges incident with \(y\). The cut inequalities imply \(g(y)\le d_B(y)\) and Hall’s condition for these slots. Their total number is \(|E(B)|\), so Hall supplies a bijection to the original edges. Select the edges assigned to \(X\)-slots. The remaining edges at \(y\) are precisely its \(d_B(y)-g(y)\) assigned edges. This proves the integral assertion. The real-valued assertion is proved separately below.

Theorem 6.2 Real max-flow/min-cut and fractional prescribed degrees
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.real_maxFlow_minCut

  • Oberwolfach.bipartite_fractionalDegrees_iff

Every finite directed network with nonnegative real capacities and distinct source and sink has a flow whose value equals the capacity of a source–sink cut. Consequently the bipartite cut criterion above is also sufficient and necessary for real edge weights \(0\le q_e\le c_e\). With capacities one, this proves the fractional assertion of the prescribed-degree lemma. The real normalized-demand application is proved with the original fixed part sizes.

Manuscript label: thm:prelim:max-flow-min-cut, lem:prelim:prescribed-degrees, lem:prelim:normalized-demands.

Proof ▼

The feasible flows form a nonempty compact subset of the capacity box, so net source outflow attains its maximum. For a fixed maximizing flow \(f\), say that \(x\) can transfer to \(y\) if some capacity-bounded \(g\) changes divergence by \(\varepsilon (\mathbf1_x-\mathbf1_y)\) for \(\varepsilon {\gt}0\). This relation is reflexive and transitive: combine transfers of sizes \(u,w\) by the convex coefficients \(w/(u+w),u/(u+w)\). A residual arc gives a transfer. The vertices reachable from the source under this relation form a cut excluding the sink; otherwise the flow value increases. Every outgoing cut arc is saturated, and every incoming cut arc has flow zero. Summing divergence over the cut proves equality of its capacity and the flow value. The three-layer bipartite network then proves the prescribed-degree criterion, including saturation of every source and sink arc. Integer integrality is supplied by the preceding Hall argument rather than assumed for real flows.

Under the normalized large-cut and demand bounds of lem:prelim:normalized-demands, the required cut inequalities hold. Consequently integer demands admit an integral subgraph. In particular, integer demands in \([q-e,q+e]\), on two equal parts of positive order, have relative error at most \(2e/(q-e)\) provided \(0\le e{\lt}q\). Here \(e\) is real and is not rounded to an integer.

Manuscript label: lem:prelim:normalized-demands.

Proof ▼

For \(s=|S|/|X|\) and \(t=|T|/|Y|\), a positive demand deficit forces \(s+t{\gt}1-2\delta _0\). The deficit is at most \((1+\delta )M\min (s,t)\), which the large-cut estimate bounds by \(e_B(S,T)\). For interval demands, their mean is at least \(q-e\) and their deviation from that mean is at most \(2e\). All these inequalities, their conversion to the integer cut condition, and the prescribed-degree application are checked.

Let \((M_a)\) partition the arcs of a balanced orientation of a simple graph, with total indegree and outdegree \(\sum _a m_a\). Suppose every colour has in- and outdegrees \(m_a\pm e\). Let \(L_a\le M_a\) be its available arcs after protected edges have been excluded. Suppose \(L_a\) satisfies the large-cut bound

\[ e_{L_a}(S,T)\ge h_a n^2\min \{ |S|/n,|T|/n\} \quad \text{if }|S|/n+|T|/n{\gt}1-2\delta _a, \]

and

\[ 0\le e{\lt}q\le \min _a m_a,\quad 0\le \delta _a{\lt}1/4,\quad \frac{2e}{q-e}\le \delta _a,\quad q+e\le h_an/2. \]

Then the underlying graph has an exact edge partition into \(2m_a\)-regular graphs. Each colour changes at most \(4q+2e\) edges at any vertex. Every edge unavailable for deletion in all colours retains its original colour.

Manuscript label: lem:family:partition.

Proof ▼

Use deletion demands \(d^+_{M_a}(v)-(m_a-q)\) and \(d^-_{M_a}(v)-(m_a-q)\) in \(L_a\). Their totals agree and the normalized lemma gives the deletion subgraphs \(T_a\). The cores have in- and outdegree \(m_a-q\). The union of the \(T_a\) has in- and outdegree \(kq\); group its perfect matchings into \(q\) per colour and add them to the cores. The proof checks exact multiplicities, physical edge coverage, both degree identities, protected colours and all four addition/deletion degree bounds. The random orientation is treated below. The complete simultaneous colour-cut event and its assembly with all remaining parameter choices are still open.

Lemma 6.5 Degree correction by directed two-edge paths
✓
#

An orientation matrix \(M\) records exactly one direction of every edge of \(G\). Let prescribed integer outdegrees \(t_v\) have total \(e(G)\) and differ from the current outdegrees by at most \(a\). Suppose every distinct ordered pair has at least \(\beta n\) eligible directed two-edge paths, and a forbidden graph has maximum degree at most \(f\). If \(L{\gt}0\) is integral and

\[ na/L+2a+4L+2f{\lt}\beta n, \]

then reversing eligible paths outside the forbidden graph gives precisely the prescribed outdegrees. At most \(a+2L\) physical edges change at a vertex.

Manuscript label: lem:simp:orientation-routing.

Proof ▼

List positive and negative degree discrepancies as paired unit requests. There are at most \(na\) requests and each vertex occurs as an endpoint at most \(a\) times. Greedily choose their middle vertices, using each at most \(L\) times. At a step, saturated middle vertices exclude at most \(na/L\) choices. Used outgoing and incoming arcs exclude at most \(2a+2L\) choices; the forbidden graph excludes at most \(2f\). The displayed stronger margin therefore leaves a choice. The chosen paths have disjoint directed arcs. Since they lie in an orientation, no two use opposite directions of one physical edge. Reversal preserves each physical-edge identity and changes outdegree by exactly the prescribed discrepancy. Counting endpoint and middle occurrences proves the stated physical degree bound.
