Theorem D.1 Near-optimal colouring with a fixed error
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma C.1
  • Lemma C.6

Lean declarations

  • Oberwolfach.uniform_improving_colour_round
  • Oberwolfach.near_optimal_hypergraph_list_colouring
  • Oberwolfach.near_optimal_hypergraph_edge_colouring

For fixed \(k\ge 1\), \(0{\lt}\epsilon \le 1/2\) and \(0{\lt}\delta \le 1\), there is \(D_0\) such that every finite indexed \(k\)-uniform hypergraph of maximum degree at most \(D\ge D_0\) and codegree at most \(D^{1-\delta }\) has a proper edge colouring with \(\lceil (1+\epsilon )D\rceil \) colours. Parallel edge indices remain distinct. A list-colouring form is proved as well.

Proof ▼

The one-round estimates give integer list sizes and degree bounds while increasing the list-to-degree ratio by a fixed positive amount. A positive fraction of each list survives each round. Both constants are chosen before the auxiliary degree bounds. A fixed finite induction maintains these bounds on the actual uncoloured edge indices, and the final local-lemma colouring extends the retained partial colouring. All floor and ceiling errors and all exponential-tail requirements are proved. No near-optimal colouring theorem is assumed.

The error \(\epsilon \) is fixed before \(D_0\). This theorem alone does not establish a power-rate error \(D^{-a}\).

Theorem D.2 Finite simultaneous weighted-matching construction

Fix nonnegative hyperedge weights \(w_a\) and put \(W_a=\sum _e w_a(e)\). Under the three explicit numerical common-event bounds in the Lean statement, there is a matching satisfying, simultaneously for every supplied test \(a\),

\[ \left|w_a(M)-\frac{W_a}{p^{k-1}q\ell }\right| {\lt} t_a+\frac{p s_a}{q\ell }, \qquad \ell =\lceil (1+\epsilon )D\rceil . \]

Here \(p\) is the number of vertex parts, \(q\) the number of edge parts, \(s_a\) the error for each first-stage part, and \(t_a\) the final selection error. The precise degree, codegree, weight and tail bounds are stated in the linked Lean theorem.

Proof ▼

One vertex-colouring outcome controls every omitted-root degree and every fixed weight sum. One subsequent edge-colouring outcome controls all part degrees, codegrees and weight totals. Proper colourings of these parts are constructed by the preceding theorem. Independent colour-class choices then give one matching satisfying every test. Projection to the original hyperedge indices is injective and preserves the selected weight sums. Summing the first-stage errors and dividing by the actual colour count gives the displayed error. All three existence steps use proved concentration inequalities and a common-event argument.

The numerical hypotheses are discharged in the fixed-error uniform theorem below. The support reduction, integer-copy and projection steps are proved in Node E.3.
